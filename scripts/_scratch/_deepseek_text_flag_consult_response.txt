### 2. Bias or variance?

The per-seed tradeoff (s0/s2 conv-strong/text-weak, s1/s5 text-strong/conv-weak) is **variance in convergence direction caused by data imbalance**, not pure variance. The model has enough capacity to separate prose, but with only 229 text steps per epoch, the random shuffle order determines whether the text direction solidifies early or gets pulled into the conv attractor. This is a **bias-variance hybrid**: the bias is the step-count deficit; the variance is the seed-dependent outcome.

**Cheap diagnostic:** For each seed, compute the **train margin-ranking loss on text-gold records** at the end of fine-tuning. If text loss is consistently higher than conv loss across all seeds, it’s bias. If text loss is low on text-strong seeds and high on text-weak seeds, it’s variance in convergence. I predict the latter — text-weak seeds will show high text loss, confirming the model failed to fit text, not that it couldn’t.

### 3. Loss-side reality check

**There is no AdamW-safe loss-side lever for this problem.** The #2 and #3 failures are definitive: loss weighting collapses under AdamW; loss-structure changes cannot compensate for representation weakness. Asymmetric margin is a loss-structure change — it alters which examples produce non-zero loss. In a regime where the representation is not yet forced to separate text from conv (due to step-count deficit), a larger margin will either be ignored (if the model can’t reach it) or will cause unstable gradients as the model lurches to satisfy it. The track record says: **treat the text flag as purely a data/seeds problem. Do not touch the loss.**
